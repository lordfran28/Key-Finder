You’ll Never Know

For your voice and piano, in C major, on the same bars and timing as the guitar arrangement. The left hand plays the bass; the right hand plays four-note shapes without the root that move as little as possible from chord to chord.

The shapes in this song

Every right-hand shape, once, with the bass under it and the bars that use it.

How the hands work

Left hand: the bass note of each chord, low, held with the pedal. On long chords in slow passages it adds the fifth above. On a slash chord (D/F♯) it plays the note after the slash.

Right hand: four notes without the root, because the left hand already has it. Every chord type has two shapes. The A shape has the 3rd at the bottom, the B shape has the 7th at the bottom. Going from one chord to the next you usually switch between them, and then most fingers stay where they are or move one key. This song uses the A shape 44 times and the B shape 26 times.

  • On the home chord the 7 becomes a 6 (3 · 5 · 6 · 9): the sixth never fights a sung root.
  • For a minor 6 chord the ♭7 becomes a 6.
  • When the chord leads to a minor chord, the 9 and 13 drop a half step (♭9, ♭13): darker, and it pulls harder.

The fills

Only where you rest at the end of a bar. Each one walks the scale of the chord it's on and stops a step away from the top note of the next chord, so that note feels like an arrival.

  • Bar V7: D melodic minor scale, 4 notes into Dm9.
  • Bar V11: G Mixolydian scale, 6 notes into G13.
  • Bar 2: G Mixolydian scale, 6 notes into C/E.

Practice order

  1. Learn the shapes at the bottom of the page one chord type at a time: say the numbers as you play them.
  2. Take one part. Left hand alone with the track, then right hand alone, then together slowly.
  3. Sing over it before the fills; add the fills last, when the chords are in your hands.
