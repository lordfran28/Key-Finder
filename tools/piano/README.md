# The piano pages

How the voice-and-piano pages on the shelf were made. Nothing here runs on the site.

- `charts.py` reads each song's guitar arrangement page (its bars, chords, timing, where the voice sings) from the Fret Finder shelf (`/home/claude/fret-finder/songs`), or from the Key Finder page itself when the guitar page isn't there. `VOICES` lists the singer's mixes still at hand; a song without one keeps the singer's track it already has on the shelf.
- `voices.py` takes the singer for each page from the guitar page's track with the singer (opened with the singer key, which stays in the session's scratchpad), takes the guitar out, and tracks the sung pitch for the rub check. Nothing it writes goes in the repository.
- `beginner.py` turns the guitar page's chart into a beginner's chart: the song's own chords played plainly (major, minor, 6th, 7th, minor 7th, half-diminished, diminished, sus; a slash chord becomes the chord it is over its root), at most one chord per half bar (one per bar in a waltz), one kind of chord per root and family for the whole song, and at most twelve chords (fifteen when the song changes key), the rarest folded into the chord beside them or the one sharing most notes, whichever sits best under the voice. `OVERRIDE` holds the few changes made by hand.
- `global_shapes.py` gives every chord one shape for the whole shelf (`shapes.json`): E7 is the same keys in every song. A song keeps its own shape for a chord only where the shared one would sit a half step from the singer.
- `arrange.py` plays the chart: a shell in the left hand (root and 7th, or root and 5th, or the root alone), three close notes in the right hand (rootless on 7th and 6th chords), the same shape every time a chord comes back, a fill only where the voice rests at the end of a bar and at most one every eight bars (twelve in fast songs), and a few short tips.
- `piano.py` plays it with the Salamander Grand Piano samples (Alexander Holm, CC BY 3.0). Put the samples in `samples/` as `A0.wav`, `C1.wav`, `Ds1.wav`, `Fs1.wav` and so on; they come from the `Salamander` folder of github.com/Tonejs/audio, decoded from mp3.
- `page.py` writes the page: keyboard diagrams labelled with chord tones, one per change ("continues" while a voicing keeps ringing; a voicing that comes back is drawn again), keys that light as they sound, the tracks.
- `build.py SLUG` does all of it for one song and writes `out/SLUG/`; `--no-audio` rewrites only the page. `build_all.sh` builds every song. The singer's track is his voice over the new piano, and goes up locked. `rubs.py SLUG` counts the seconds where a piano note sits a half step from the singer; `show.py SLUG` prints the chart before and after, bar by bar, with what is sung.
- `faure.py` reads Après un rêve from the OpenScore Lieder score (CC0); `faure_beginner.py` makes its page from Fauré's harmony the same way, played as his right hand plays it: three soft notes to the beat.
- `import_all.sh SLUG ...` puts built songs on the shelf with `tools/import_arrangement.py`.
- `design.py` makes the site's velvet (light and dark) from the Met velvet in Valve Finder's `assets/source`; `heroes.py DIR` cuts the title paintings of Key Finder and Fret Finder (Ragueneau's harpsichordist, Watteau's *Mezzetin*) from the originals in DIR.
