About this song

Gabriel Fauré wrote Après un rêve around 1877 to a poem by Romain Bussine, after an anonymous Tuscan poem. It is the first of his Three Songs, Op. 7, published in 1878. The words: a sleeper dreams of the one he loves, they fly toward the light together, and he wakes and begs the night to give the dream back.

How the piano part works

The right hand repeats each chord in three even notes to the beat (triplets), soft and steady, while the left hand holds the bass, often walking down by step. The singer floats over it in long, slow phrases. Keep the right hand light and even, and let the left hand carry the line.

The page shows Fauré's notes exactly, moved down a minor third from his C minor into A minor, the key of the low-voice editions. Each diagram is a chord as it sounds for one or more beats; play its right-hand notes as the three repeated notes of each beat.

What to listen for

  • The bass falls by half steps under the first lines: each new chord is only one key away from the last.
  • Many chords are seventh chords that lean on the next one (dominant sevenths and their relatives): that's what gives the song its endless, dreaming pull.
  • At the climax, the voice reaches its highest note while the piano grows; then everything falls back to the quiet opening chord.

Practice order

  1. Left hand alone with the track, one part at a time.
  2. Right hand alone, slowly, as plain chords on the beat; then in triplets.
  3. Together, then sing over it with the "Piano and the tune" track until you know the line.
