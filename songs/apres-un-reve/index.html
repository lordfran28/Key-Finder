Voice and piano · A minor

Après un rêve

Fauré’s harmony, a minor third down in A minor, the key of the low-voice editions; you sing the line an octave below what is written there. 12 chords, each with one shape that stays the same every time it comes back, played as Fauré’s right hand plays them: three soft notes to the beat. Romain Bussine’s words are above.

The shapes in this song

Every right-hand shape, once, with the bass under it and the bars that use it.

How the hands work

Each chord has one shape for the whole song. When a chord comes back, your hands go back to the same keys.

Left hand: a shell, the root with the 7th (or the 5th) above it, low and held with the pedal.

Right hand: three notes close together. On 7th and 6th chords the root is left out, because the left hand has it.

The chords are the song’s own, played plainly. Fauré’s passing chords are left out; his right hand’s three soft notes to the beat are kept.

The fills

Only where you rest at the end of a bar, and no more than one in eight bars.

  • Bar 15: C major scale, 4 notes into E7.

Practice order

  1. The 12 shapes on their own, one hand and then both, until you can find each without looking.
  2. Left hand alone with the track, then right hand alone, then together, slowly.
  3. Sing over it before the fills; add the fills last.
